## About this Sheet

AI-focused funds are not a monolith—they range from generalist firms that have simply gotten loud about AI to deeply technical shops run by former ML researchers and infrastructure engineers. The distinction matters. Technical GPs will grill you on model architecture, training data provenance, and inference costs; they&apos;ll know if you&apos;re wrapping an API or building something defensible. Generalist funds riding the AI wave tend to care more about market size and go-to-market, which can work in your favor if your moat is distribution rather than pure tech. Before you pitch, figure out which type you&apos;re talking to. A quick scan of the partners&apos; backgrounds (did they ship ML systems, or did they invest in a few AI deals from a generalist seat?) tells you how to calibrate your deck.
​
One dynamic worth understanding: AI-focused funds see an overwhelming volume of pitches right now, which means they&apos;ve developed sharper filters and shorter attention spans for anything that feels like a thin wrapper on foundation models. If your defensibility story is "we fine-tuned GPT for X vertical," you need a very compelling answer on what happens when OpenAI or a competitor launches that feature natively. The funds on this list have seen hundreds of those pitches—they&apos;re looking for proprietary data flywheels, novel model architectures, or deep workflow integration that makes you sticky. Also know that many AI-specialist funds have strong opinions on build-vs-buy for compute and infrastructure, so be ready to discuss your technical roadmap in detail, not just your product roadmap.
