# What Clinical Trial Venture Capital Think About Clinical Trial Tech

By Moe Alsumidaie

September 2, 2025

“Even when the ROI is 10 to 13x, it still takes six years to adopt.” That’s the paradox that Sunny Kumar, Partner at Informed Ventures, grapples with daily. His firm has backed next-generation clinical trial platforms like Medable and Deep6 AI. But the question remains: why does it take decades for these innovations to become standard practice?

In this interview, Kumar unpacks the behavioral biases, structural bottlenecks, and regulatory shifts shaping the adoption of decentralized trials and AI. He shares lessons from the recent DCT contraction, offers real examples of sites opting out due to tech burden, and explains why the term “DCT” itself may not survive the next five years.



## Moe: Sunny, you’ve backed platforms like Medable and Deep6 AI. But Tufts data shows it still takes 6 years for companies to adopt tech and 20 for widespread uptake. Why the delay?

Sunny: Pharma and healthcare are fundamentally risk-averse. They’re not early adopters by nature. The stakes are too high—no one wants to jeopardize a phase 3 trial using unproven tech. That caution is understandable but also slows innovation. DCTs are a great example. Medable is a newer platform, but decentralized elements have been around for over 20 years. It’s only recently, thanks to FDA guidance and hard ROI data—like 10x to 13x returns per Tufts—that adoption is gaining steam. Still, it’s not plug-and-play. You need upgraded data lakes, pipelines, and backend support. That infrastructure lift is a real barrier.

## Moe: LinkedIn data shows DCT headcounts are dropping even as the market grows. Is this a correction or something deeper?

Sunny: It’s mostly a correction. Post- COVID , VCs like us expected hypergrowth—3x, 4x, even 5x year-over-year. Companies staffed up aggressively to meet those projections. But reality hit: sales cycles in clinical trials are long, and sustainable growth looks more like 2x. The smart companies have adjusted. They cut back, recalibrated, and are now better staffed to achieve profitability. This isn’t a sign that DCTs are failing. It’s a sign the sector is maturing.



## Moe: DCTs promised democratization. But many sites decline participation. What are you seeing at the ground level?

Sunny: Real-world data supports DCTs: better retention, broader reach, underserved populations included. But execution is tough. Take device provisioning. Pharma companies aren’t built to ship and support devices. Sites aren’t trained for remote workflows. Some say no outright because the training burden, tech stack, and backend support are too much.

One initiative we saw failed because translation and localization alone spanned 50+ countries. These aren’t edge cases—they’re systemic blockers. But this is where AI can help: translation, CRA support, automating workflow redundancies. Agentic AI is the next frontier.

## Moe: The FDA’s tone has shifted: less emphasis on diversity targets, more on resilience and adaptability. How are startups responding?

Sunny: That shift matters. Everyone agrees diversity is important, but resilience is the new north star. If trials are adaptive and robust, we get better outcomes faster. Startups are responding in kind. Whether it means in silico control arms, fully virtual trials, or adaptive designs, the focus now is on operational flexibility. That changes how startups pitch, what they prioritize in product development, and how they build toward regulatory alignment.

## Moe: Let’s talk AI. It has enormous promise but also risk. Have you seen well-intentioned tools worsen inequity?

Sunny: Absolutely. Early big data efforts often hardcoded physician bias into the algorithms. Without safeguards, AI can deepen the very gaps we aim to close. What we need is inclusivity by design: ensuring rural areas have connectivity, investing in digital literacy, training staff across sites—not just at major hubs. The goal isn’t a perfectly equal field. It’s to up-level everyone.



## Moe: Some leaders now say we should stop calling it “DCT” and just call it a trial. What’s the legacy of the DCT era?

Sunny: That shift is already happening. DCT elements—eConsent, eCOA, remote monitoring—are being baked into trials by default. Soon we won’t distinguish between “DCT” and “trial.” That’s the legacy: not just tech adoption, but a cultural shift in how trials are run.

## Moe: Anything else you’d like to add?

Sunny: I’m most excited about AI agents. The automation we’re starting to see—in CRA support, document translation, backend workflows—is just the beginning. The ones that succeed will be those who build for flexibility, automation, and inclusivity from day one.
