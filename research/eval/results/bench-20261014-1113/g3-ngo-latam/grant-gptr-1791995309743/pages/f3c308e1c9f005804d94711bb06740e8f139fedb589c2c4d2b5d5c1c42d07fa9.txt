# Packard Foundation Grants 2026: Complete Guide to Maternal & Child Health Funding Opportunities

The David & Lucile Packard Foundation is one of the world's leading philanthropic organizations supporting maternal and child health, reproductive health, environmental conservation, and community well-being. Through strategic grantmaking, the Foundation partners with nonprofit organizations, academic institutions, healthcare providers, and community-led initiatives to improve health outcomes and create lasting social impact.

Unlike many traditional grantmakers that focus on short-term funding cycles, the Packard Foundation emphasizes long-term partnerships, community leadership, measurable outcomes, and sustainable solutions. Its investments help organizations strengthen healthcare access, improve reproductive health services, advance early childhood development, and build healthier communities across the United States and selected international regions.

If your organization plans to apply for Packard Foundation Grants in 2026, understanding the Foundation's priorities, funding approach, eligibility requirements, and proposal expectations can significantly improve your chances of success.

## About the David & Lucile Packard Foundation

Founded in 1964, the David & Lucile Packard Foundation is a private philanthropic organization committed to improving the lives of children, families, and communities. The Foundation supports initiatives that advance maternal and child health, reproductive health, environmental conservation, children's well-being, and community resilience through evidence-based, community-driven solutions.

Working alongside nonprofits, healthcare organizations, researchers, public agencies, and local leaders, the Foundation invests in programs that address systemic challenges and create measurable, sustainable impact. Its grantmaking spans both domestic and international initiatives, with projects supporting healthier communities across the United States and in selected regions in Africa, Asia, and Latin America.



## Packard Foundation Funding Priorities for 2026

The Packard Foundation supports organizations working to improve health equity, strengthen community systems, and deliver lasting social impact. While individual funding opportunities vary, successful applicants typically align their projects with one or more of the Foundation's strategic priorities.



### Maternal & Child Health

Maternal and child health remains one of the Foundation's most significant areas of investment. Funding supports programs that improve health outcomes before, during, and after pregnancy while expanding access to quality healthcare for mothers, infants, and young children.

Projects may focus on:


Prenatal and postnatal care

Maternal nutrition

Infant health and development

Early childhood health programs

Perinatal care coordination

Family health education

Rural maternal healthcare

Community-based maternal health initiatives

Health equity for underserved populations


Organizations that demonstrate measurable improvements in maternal and infant outcomes through evidence-based and community-led approaches are often well aligned with the Foundation's priorities.



### Reproductive Health & Rights

The Foundation has a longstanding commitment to advancing reproductive health and rights through equitable access to healthcare, education, and policy development.

Funding may support initiatives related to:


Family planning services

Reproductive healthcare access

Women's health programs

Youth reproductive health education

Community outreach and education

Healthcare workforce development

Evidence-based reproductive health research

Policy and advocacy initiatives


Successful projects typically combine healthcare delivery, education, and community engagement to improve reproductive health outcomes while addressing barriers to care.



### Community-Led Health Initiatives

Community leadership is central to the Foundation's grantmaking philosophy. Rather than promoting one-size-fits-all solutions, the Foundation prioritizes programs developed in collaboration with local communities that reflect their unique needs and lived experiences.

Examples of supported initiatives include:


Community health worker programs

Local maternal health partnerships

Women's leadership initiatives

Community outreach programs

Rural healthcare access projects

Cross-sector collaborations

Regional public health partnerships


Programs that actively involve community members throughout planning, implementation, and evaluation often demonstrate stronger long-term sustainability and impact.



### Environmental Health & Climate Resilience

The Foundation recognizes the strong connection between environmental conditions and community health. Funding may support initiatives that improve environmental sustainability while protecting the health of women, children, and vulnerable populations.

Priority areas may include:


Environmental justice

Clean air and water initiatives

Climate resilience planning

Sustainable community development

Healthy neighborhood initiatives

Climate-related health risk reduction

Conservation efforts that improve public health


Organizations proposing interdisciplinary projects that combine environmental sustainability with public health improvements may find strong alignment with the Foundation's long-term objectives.





## Who Can Apply for Packard Foundation Grants?

The Packard Foundation supports organizations that demonstrate strong community partnerships, measurable impact, and the ability to deliver sustainable health improvements. Eligibility varies by funding opportunity, but grants are commonly awarded to organizations working in maternal health, reproductive health, children's well-being, environmental conservation, and community development.

Eligible applicants may include:


Nonprofit organizations

Community-based organizations (CBOs)

Healthcare providers and health systems

Academic institutions and universities

Public health agencies

Research organizations

Tribal organizations (where applicable)

Regional coalitions and collaborative partnerships

International NGOs (for eligible global programs)


Many funding opportunities encourage collaboration among healthcare providers, researchers, community leaders, government agencies, and nonprofit organizations to maximize long-term impact.

Organizations should carefully review each Request for Proposals (RFP) or funding announcement, as eligibility requirements and geographic focus areas differ by program.

## Understanding the Application Process

Although each funding opportunity has unique requirements, most applications follow a structured process.


Step 1: Identify the Appropriate Funding Opportunity

Review available grant programs and determine whether your organization's mission, geographic focus, and project objectives align with the Foundation's funding priorities.

Applicants should ensure their proposed initiative clearly supports one or more strategic areas such as maternal health, reproductive health, community leadership, children's well-being, or environmental sustainability.


Step 2: Confirm Eligibility Requirements

Before investing significant time in proposal development, verify that your organization satisfies all eligibility criteria outlined in the funding announcement.

Depending on the opportunity, this may include:


Organizational status

Geographic eligibility

Program focus

Partnership requirements

Prior funding history

Financial and compliance documentation



Step 3: Develop a Strong Project Proposal

Competitive proposals clearly explain:


The community challenge being addressed

Evidence supporting the proposed approach

Project goals and measurable objectives

Target population

Community engagement strategy

Implementation plan

Evaluation methodology

Sustainability beyond the grant period


Reviewers generally favor proposals that demonstrate strong partnerships, realistic implementation timelines, and measurable outcomes rather than broad or theoretical concepts.


Step 4: Prepare Supporting Documentation

Depending on the funding opportunity, applicants may be asked to provide supporting documents such as:


Organizational information

Program narrative

Budget and budget justification

Project timeline

Letters of support

Leadership information

Evaluation framework

Financial documentation


Submitting complete and well-organized materials helps streamline the review process and demonstrates organizational readiness.


Step 5: Submit the Application

Applications may be submitted through an online grants portal, by invitation, or in response to an approved Letter of Inquiry (LOI), depending on the specific funding opportunity.

Organizations should review all submission requirements carefully and submit well before deadlines to allow time for technical or administrative corrections.



## How the Packard Foundation Evaluates Grant Proposals

The Packard Foundation evaluates proposals based on how well they align with its strategic priorities, demonstrate measurable impact, and strengthen communities through sustainable solutions. While review criteria vary by program, successful applications typically address several common evaluation areas.



### Strategic Alignment

Reviewers assess whether the proposed project directly supports the Foundation's funding priorities, including maternal and child health, reproductive health and rights, community-led solutions, environmental sustainability, and early childhood development.

Projects that clearly explain how their goals contribute to long-term community well-being generally receive stronger consideration.



### Community Leadership and Partnerships

The Foundation places significant value on initiatives that are designed and led in partnership with the communities they serve.

Competitive proposals often demonstrate:


Strong community engagement

Meaningful stakeholder participation

Cross-sector collaboration

Partnerships with healthcare providers, nonprofits, public agencies, or educational institutions

Local leadership throughout project implementation


Programs that reflect community priorities rather than externally developed solutions often align well with the Foundation's approach.



### Evidence-Based Program Design

Successful applications clearly explain why the proposed intervention is likely to achieve meaningful results.

Reviewers typically look for:


Data supporting the identified need

Research-informed program strategies

Clearly defined objectives

Practical implementation methods

Realistic timelines

Risk mitigation plans


Organizations should demonstrate both the need for the project and the feasibility of delivering measurable outcomes.



### Measurement and Evaluation

The Foundation expects applicants to define how success will be measured.

Strong proposals include:


Performance indicators

Outcome metrics

Evaluation methodology

Data collection processes

Reporting plans

Continuous improvement strategies


Applicants should explain how evaluation findings will be used to strengthen programs and improve long-term impact.



### Organizational Capacity

Reviewers assess whether the organization has the leadership, financial management, staffing, partnerships, and operational infrastructure necessary to manage grant funding successfully.

Applicants should demonstrate:


Relevant experience

Qualified leadership

Financial accountability

Project management capability

Long-term sustainability


Organizations with a history of successful community programs and collaborative partnerships often present stronger applications.



## Common Reasons Grant Applications Are Not Funded

Many proposals meet basic eligibility requirements but are not selected because they fail to demonstrate sufficient impact or implementation readiness.

Common reasons include:


Weak alignment with Foundation priorities

Limited community involvement

Broad or unclear project objectives

Insufficient evidence supporting the proposed approach

Poorly defined outcome measures

Weak evaluation plans

Limited sustainability beyond grant funding

Unrealistic budgets or timelines

Incomplete supporting documentation


Addressing these issues before submission can significantly strengthen a proposal.



## Tips for Writing a Competitive Packard Foundation Proposal

Organizations preparing proposals for future funding opportunities should focus on demonstrating long-term community impact rather than simply describing program activities.

Competitive proposals typically:


Clearly define the community challenge being addressed.

Explain why the proposed solution is evidence-based.

Demonstrate meaningful community leadership.

Include measurable goals and evaluation metrics.

Show collaboration across multiple organizations.

Present realistic implementation timelines.

Explain how the project will continue after grant funding ends.

Provide transparent and well-justified budgets.

Describe expected long-term outcomes for families and communities.


Reviewers generally favor projects that combine innovation with practical implementation and measurable results.
