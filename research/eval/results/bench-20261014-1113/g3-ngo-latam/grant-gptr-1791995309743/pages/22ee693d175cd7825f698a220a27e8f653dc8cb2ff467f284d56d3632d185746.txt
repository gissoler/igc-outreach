# Rural Community Health Support Program: $4.5M for Rural Healthcare Access

The Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA), part of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, is offering $4.5 million through the Rural Community Health Support Program. This cooperative agreement funds nationally available technical assistance to help community-based organizations and rural health stakeholders improve the delivery of and access to quality healthcare in rural communities across the country.

Rural communities face persistent challenges in healthcare access, including provider shortages, limited infrastructure, and geographic isolation. This program addresses those gaps by supporting coordinated technical assistance that strengthens the capacity of local organizations to deliver effective health services where they are needed most.

## What Is the Rural Community Health Support Program?

The Rural Community Health Support Program is designed to build and sustain a national infrastructure of technical assistance for rural health organizations. Rather than funding direct healthcare services, this program supports the development and coordination of resources, training, and guidance that help rural communities strengthen their own healthcare delivery systems.

The recipient of this cooperative agreement will be expected to develop, deliver, and coordinate technical assistance that is available nationwide. This includes working with community-based organizations, local and tribal governments, healthcare providers, and other rural health stakeholders to identify needs, share best practices, and implement strategies that improve access to quality care in underserved rural areas.

Because rural health challenges vary widely across regions, the program emphasizes flexible, community-driven approaches. Technical assistance may cover areas such as workforce development, care coordination, health program funding strategies , telehealth adoption, and organizational capacity building.

## Who Can Apply?

This program has broad eligibility, reflecting the wide range of organizations that play a role in rural healthcare delivery. Eligible applicants include:

State governments and state agencies

Local governments including cities, counties, townships, and special district governments

Federally recognized Tribal governments and Tribal organizations

Nonprofit organizations with and without 501(c)(3) status

Educational institutions including public and private universities and colleges

For-profit organizations and small businesses

Public and Indian housing authorities

The wide eligibility criteria mean that organizations already embedded in rural communities, whether they are local health departments, nonprofits, universities, or tribal health programs, are well-positioned to apply. Applicants should demonstrate experience working with rural populations and the capacity to deliver technical assistance at a national scale.

## What Does the Program Fund?

The $4.5 million cooperative agreement supports a comprehensive scope of work centered on technical assistance for rural health improvement. Key funded activities include:

### Technical Assistance Development and Delivery

Creating training materials, toolkits, and resource guides tailored to rural health challenges

Delivering webinars, workshops, and one-on-one consultations for rural health stakeholders

Providing guidance on healthcare delivery models that work in low-density, geographically isolated settings

### Healthcare Coordination

Facilitating partnerships between rural healthcare providers, community organizations, and government agencies

Supporting care coordination strategies that connect rural residents with appropriate services

Promoting integration of behavioral health, primary care, and social services in rural settings

### Rural Health Improvement

Identifying and disseminating evidence-based practices for improving rural health outcomes

Supporting community health assessments and strategic planning for rural organizations

Assisting with quality improvement initiatives and performance measurement in rural healthcare settings

## Grant vs. Cooperative Agreement

This funding opportunity is structured as a cooperative agreement rather than a traditional grant. While both provide federal funding to support a defined scope of work, the key difference lies in the level of federal involvement.

With a standard grant, the recipient operates with substantial independence once the award is made. A cooperative agreement, by contrast, involves substantial federal programmatic involvement throughout the project period. This means HRSA will actively participate in planning, guiding, and coordinating the funded activities alongside the recipient organization.

In practice, this means applicants should expect:

Regular collaboration and communication with HRSA program staff

Federal input on technical assistance strategies and priorities

Joint decision-making on key program directions and deliverables

Ongoing federal review of progress and outcomes

This collaborative structure reflects the national scope of the program and HRSA's interest in ensuring that technical assistance efforts align with broader federal rural health priorities.

## How to Apply

Applications must be submitted through Grants.gov before the July 8, 2026 at 11:59 PM ET deadline. Here are the key steps to prepare your application:

Review the full Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) - Search for opportunity number HRSA-26-083 on Grants.gov to access the complete application instructions and requirements

Verify your SAM.gov registration - Ensure your organization has an active Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) and current SAM.gov registration . Registration can take several weeks, so start early

Register on Grants.gov - If your organization is not already registered, complete the Grants.gov registration process well before the deadline

Prepare your application narrative - Address all required sections outlined in the NOFO, including your approach to delivering national technical assistance, organizational capacity, and evaluation plan

Compile required documents - Gather all mandatory forms, budget narratives, letters of support, and organizational documentation

Submit by July 8, 2026 at 11:59 PM ET - Allow time for Grants.gov processing and potential technical issues. Late submissions will not be accepted
