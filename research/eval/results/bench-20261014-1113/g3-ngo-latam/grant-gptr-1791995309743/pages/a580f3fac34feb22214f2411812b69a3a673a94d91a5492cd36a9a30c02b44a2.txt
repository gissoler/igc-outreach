## Can you apply?

This grant is for eligible healthcare organizations that can demonstrate capacity to establish, expand, or enhance telehealth infrastructure and services serving underserved populations. Applicants typically include community health centers, critical access hospitals, rural health clinics, federally qualified health centers (FQHCs), and other safety-net providers. Geographic preference is given to organizations serving rural, frontier, and underserved urban areas. The program supports telehealth service delivery, workforce training and capacity building, technology infrastructure development, and activities that improve access to specialty care and behavioral health services through telehealth. Organizations must be able to commit to sustainability planning and demonstrate community need for expanded telehealth services.

### Program description

The Telehealth Centers of Excellence program will support academic medical centers in implementing and evaluating innovative telehealth solutions in rural and underserved communities. These centers will serve as national models by testing concepts and creating proven approaches that expand access to care and improve health outcomes and can be replicated nationally.

## FAQ

Who is eligible to apply for the Telehealth Centers of Excellence grant?
Typically, eligible applicants include FQHCs, rural health clinics, critical access hospitals, community health centers, and other safety-net healthcare providers. Your organization must demonstrate the capacity and infrastructure to establish or expand telehealth services and have a clear mission to serve underserved populations.

What types of telehealth activities are supported by this grant?
Common supported activities include developing telehealth delivery systems, expanding access to specialty care and mental health services, training and credentialing telehealth workforce, purchasing technology infrastructure and equipment, and establishing sustainability plans for telehealth programs.

When is the application deadline?
The specific deadline has not been announced yet. Historically, HRSA programs release detailed Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) documents on Grants.gov well in advance of the deadline. Monitor Grants.gov and the HRSA website regularly for updates.

What makes a competitive application?
Strong applications typically demonstrate clear community need, specific measurable outcomes, a realistic budget, demonstrated organizational capacity, partnership agreements with specialists or other providers, and a detailed sustainability plan beyond the grant period.

What is the typical funding range for this program?
HRSA telehealth grants typically range from $100,000 to $500,000 annually, though this varies by the specific NOFO. Check the final program announcement for exact award amounts and project period length.

### 💡 Tips for applicants

Start early by monitoring Grants.gov and HRSA's website for the Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO), which will contain specific eligibility criteria, application requirements, and evaluation priorities that differ from general guidance.

Build strong partnerships before applying: demonstrate relationships with specialists, other providers, or academic institutions that will support your telehealth network and ensure diverse access points for rural or underserved communities.

Develop detailed sustainability planning in your narrative that shows how telehealth services will continue and grow financially after the grant period ends—reviewers prioritize organizations with realistic revenue models.

Use data to tell your story: include current utilization metrics, community health needs assessments, projected patient outcomes, and demographic data showing who will benefit, especially vulnerable populations and those with limited healthcare access.

Invest time in a realistic, well-documented budget narrative that aligns with proposed activities; separate costs clearly between personnel, equipment, technology, training, and evaluation, and explain how each expense supports your telehealth expansion goals.

### ⚠️ Common mistakes

Common reasons applications are rejected include lack of evidence of community need or insufficient baseline data to justify the proposed telehealth expansion. Organizations often underestimate the importance of comprehensive sustainability planning and fail to demonstrate realistic pathways for maintaining services without grant funding. Additionally, weak partnership documentation or unclear roles of partners significantly weakens competitiveness; reviewers expect evidence that collaborating providers will actively support and refer to your telehealth services.
