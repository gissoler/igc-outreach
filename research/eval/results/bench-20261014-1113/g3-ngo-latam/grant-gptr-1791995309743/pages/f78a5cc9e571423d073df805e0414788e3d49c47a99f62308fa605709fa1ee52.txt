IOM Vision

In partnership with government institutions, UN agencies, civil society, and local communities, IOM Colombia will strengthen institutional and community capacities for anticipatory action, preparedness, and crisis response, reducing risks and vulnerabilities of populations affected by armed conflict, disasters, and human mobility. IOM will provide life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection interventions while advancing durable solutions, peacebuilding, and stabilization. Further, IOM will ensure comprehensive support to IDPs and international migrants, promoting regular pathways, social cohesion, and inclusive integration in host communities. By aligning with government and inter-agency frameworks, IOM seeks to foster resilience, sustainable recovery, and effective migration governance that benefits all.


IOM Added value

IOM has extensive experience addressing migration, displacement, conflict, peacebuilding, and disaster response in Colombia. Recognized as a trusted partner by communities and authorities, IOM leverages its broad operational footprint and technical expertise to advance the humanitarian-peace-development nexus through coordinated, inclusive interventions that strengthen institutional capacities and foster sustainable recovery nationwide.

Colombia’s humanitarian situation has considerably worsened due to the convergence of armed conflict, the impacts of climate and environmental hazards, and mixed migration flows. While the signing of the 2016 Peace Agreement initially reduced violence, new and reconfigured armed actors have expanded their presence, driving escalating levels of displacement, civilian harm, and humanitarian needs across multiple regions of the country.
By August 2025, the number of people affected by armed conflict had increased by 235 per cent compared to the same period in 2024 ( IOM, 2025 ). Civilian attacks rose by nearly 25 per cent, while mass displacement reached more than 83,700 people, representing a 94 per cent increase from 2024 ( OCHA, 2025 ). An additional 103,000 people were affected by individual displacement events, and more than 879,000 faced mobility restrictions. Disproportionate impacts were recorded among ethnic communities: 26 per cent of those affected were Afro-Colombian and 41 per cent Indigenous ( OCHA, 2025 ). Departments such as Cauca, Chocó, and Norte de Santander remain the most severely impacted, with the Catatumbo subregion alone registering over 62,000 people displaced and 27,000 confined (confinement occurs when communities face severe movement and access restrictions—typically imposed by non-state armed groups—preventing access to three or more essential goods or services for at least one week) in the first half of 2025 ( Global Protection Cluster ).
The conflict’s impact on children has intensified. Between January and August 2025, at least 11,000 children were directly affected by mass displacement, confinement, or mobility restrictions, though underreporting suggests the true figure is closer to 291,400 ( OCHA, 2025 ). Grave human rights violations remain widespread: the UN verified 646 serious violations against 513 children in 2024, including killings, maiming, and sexual violence ( Secretary-General Annual Report on Children and Armed Conflict, 2024 ). Recruitment has surged by 64 per cent compared to 2023, with non-governmental organization (NGO) networks estimating that more than 1,700 children were recruited or used in 2024 (“use” refers to children’s involvement in supporting armed actors without formal recruitment. “Utilization” covers all forms of exploitation, including combat, sexual or labour abuse, and illicit activities. Both are defined in national law as grave child rights violations) ( Foro de ONG Humanitarias 2024 ). Schools and hospitals continue to be targeted, with 42 attacks reported in 2024, leading to complete evacuations ( Secretary-General Annual Report on Children and Armed Conflict, 2024 ).
Explosive hazards also pose a mounting risk. Between 2021 and 2024, incidents involving anti-personnel mines and unexploded ordnance increased tenfold—from 1,069 to 11,498—placing over 607,000 people at risk of accidents, displacement, or confinement ( Foro de ONG Humanitarias 2024 ). Moreover, by August 2025, the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reported 31,593 people affected by attacks on civilian infrastructure, mainly schools and health facilities—a staggering 5,600 per cent increase compared to 2024.
Compounding these challenges are the growing effects of climate change, a trend that has increased in later years: the number of people affected by disasters in 2024 rose by 417 per cent compared to 2023 ( National Unit for Disaster Risk Management, 2024 ). By September 2025, more than 815,000 people were affected by floods, landslides, and wildfires ( OCHA, 2025 ), with rural and marginalized communities bearing the brunt. Colombia remains severely exposed to disasters, environmental degradation and the adverse impacts of climate change, a situation compounded by inequalities and weaknesses in land planning and disaster risk reduction. Colombia also remains the world’s deadliest country for land and environmental defenders, with a third of all lethal attacks globally documented occurring in the country in 2024 ( Global Witness, 2025 ).
On 10 August, a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck western and central Colombia, causing significant loss of life and injuries, with many people reported missing. The disaster has damaged homes and disrupted critical infrastructure and basic services, including water, electricity, health care, education, and telecommunications, further increasing humanitarian needs.
Migration dynamics further intensify humanitarian pressures. As of June 2025, Colombia hosts 2.82 million Venezuelan refugees and migrants, including nearly 476,000 without regularized status ( Migración Colombia, 2025 ). During the first nine months of 2025, Colombia registered 240,000 returns and 225,000 entries from Venezuela, with 89 per cent identifying Colombia as their final destination. Flows across Ecuador’s border reached 339,000 movements (between January and August 2025), a 73 per cent increase from 2024, while 16,000 entries were recorded at the Panama border, most of Venezuelan nationality and originating from Mexico. New patterns show that 26 per cent of those arriving via Panama intend to remain in Colombia, while 68 per cent plan to return to Venezuela ( IOM, 2025 ). Southbound migration flows have also gained traction. While northbound crossings through the Darién continue (84,000 between January-July 2025) ( Migración Colombia ), growing numbers of Venezuelan, Haitian, and Ecuadorian migrants are moving southward due to restrictive migration policies in North and Central America. These flows face acute humanitarian needs, with 92 per cent requiring food, transportation, or livelihoods support ( R4V, 2025 ).

Colombia’s humanitarian coordination architecture currently includes two main mechanisms: the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), led by OCHA, which oversees responses to internal displacement, and the Interagency Group on Mixed Migration Flows (GIFMM, by its in Spanish acronym), co-led by IOM and the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), which coordinates assistance for international migrants, Colombian returnees, and host communities. Within the framework of the humanitarian reset, OCHA, the Resident Coordinator’s Office (RCO), UNHCR, and IOM are working to adapt coordination structures to ensure integrated responses to crises stemming from armed conflict, disasters, climate impacts, and migration. A key milestone has been the integration of OCHA and GIFMM coordination through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) and the Information Management Working Group (IMWG), which strengthens joint analysis and harmonized responses at both the national and territorial levels. IOM Colombia plays a central role across these mechanisms. It co-leads the GIFMM at the national and subnational levels and co-leads its Shelter and Humanitarian Transportation sector and coordinates the Counter-Trafficking Area of Responsibility (AoR). Within the HCT, IOM leads the merged Shelter and Settlements (S&S) and Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster. IOM’s leadership ensures that humanitarian response efforts integrate cross-cutting approaches and align with peacebuilding and development objectives.
Beyond the humanitarian architecture, IOM engages actively in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and has aligned its programming with the United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework ( UNSDCF 2024–2027 ). Under the UNSDCF, IOM co-leads Axis 3, which focuses on social protection, migration management, and institutional capacity strengthening for crisis response. Through this role, IOM works to strengthen governmental institutions, local authorities, and community actors to better manage emergencies and guarantee access to essential services to migrants.

IOM also participates in the UNSDCF’s territorialization process, which is piloting localized cooperation frameworks in priority regions. Through its local presence, IOM provides evidence and technical expertise to ensure that these pilots reflect local realities, particularly in territories severely affected by displacement, migration, and climate shocks.

For over 25 years, the IOM Country Office in Colombia has supported the Government of Colombia (GoC) and national stakeholders in addressing migration-related challenges across preparedness, emergency response, recovery, stabilization, peacebuilding, and durable solutions. With its main office in Bogotá and three sub-offices, and operations spanning 31 departments and 289 municipalities in 2024, IOM maintains a strong field presence. This wide reach allows the Organization to provide timely assistance and protection to migrants, refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, while ensuring that interventions are tailored to diverse territorial contexts. IOM’s engagement goes beyond direct implementation, as it plays a critical role in decision-making and coordination with local authorities, national institutions, UN agencies, and civil society actors. At the regional level, IOM Colombia plays a pivotal role in coordinating migration management efforts, particularly through the Regional Interagency Coordination Platform for Refugees and Migrants from Venezuela (R4V). Leveraging its technical expertise, operational capacities, and conflict-sensitive approach, IOM works in close collaboration with the GoC and international partners to address the Venezuelan refugee and migrant crisis. 
IOM Colombia’s mission is fully aligned with the Organization’s Strategic Results Framework (SRF) and consistently applies a conflict-sensitive lens in all phases of its interventions. This ensures that programming not only prevents harm but actively maximizes positive impacts by fostering resilience, strengthening social cohesion, and addressing root causes of conflict and displacement. Within Colombia’s complex and dynamic context, this approach allows IOM to design and implement programmes that bridge humanitarian relief, development, and peacebuilding.
Objective 1 – Saving Lives and Protecting People on the Move

IOM co-leads the Shelter and Settlements (S&S)/CCCM Cluster and the Shelter and Humanitarian Transportation sector of the GIFMM, ensuring coordinated, climate-resilient, and community-based responses. It delivers emergency shelter, rental assistance, non-food items, and cash-based interventions, while supporting public health through primary healthcare, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), life-saving protection interventions, gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response, and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

Objective 1 – Saving Lives and Protecting People on the Move
IOM co-leads the Shelter and Settlements (S&S)/CCCM Cluster and the Shelter and Humanitarian Transportation sector of the GIFMM, ensuring coordinated, climate-resilient, and community-based responses. It delivers emergency shelter, rental assistance, non-food items, and cash-based interventions, while supporting public health through primary healthcare, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), life-saving protection interventions, gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response, and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. IOM also implements the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) to monitor flows, assess risks, and produce early warnings that strengthen preparedness and decision-making.
Objective 2 – Driving Solutions to Displacement
IOM supports preparedness efforts, peace consolidation and displacement prevention by strengthening national and local institutions under the Peace Agreement and Victims’ Law. It partners with the GoC to advance collective reparation, transitional justice, and monitoring systems for peace implementation. At the community level, IOM fosters resilience and stabilization, supports rural development and climate adaptation, and promotes disaster risk reduction, including 55 energy transition projects across 12 departments. Through the Internal Displacement Solutions Fund (IDSF), IOM leads participatory Durable Solutions Plans in seven cities, focused on housing, livelihoods, and education. IOM also participates in policy discussions surrounding the nexus between disasters, environmental hazards and human mobility.
Objective 3 – Facilitating Pathways for Regular Migration
IOM assists the GoC in expanding regular migration pathways, including the Temporary Protection Statute for Venezuelans. It promotes labour migration through bilateral agreements and supports assisted voluntary return and reintegration (AVRR) for Colombians returning from other countries . IOM also strengthens state capacities to prevent and respond to trafficking in persons and migrant smuggling, combining institutional training with community awareness. Through its Immigration and Border Governance (IBG) strategy, IOM provides technical and operational assistance to ensure safe, orderly, and rights-based border management.

The ongoing armed conflict and overlapping crises in Colombia continue to generate complex humanitarian challenges, particularly regarding the protection of victims and displaced populations. In this context, IOM plays a vital role in supporting the Government of Colombia (GoC) to design and implement policies that combine immediate humanitarian responses with long-term recovery, peacebuilding, and migration management. IOM works closely with the Victims’ Unit to strengthen the Victims’ Public Policy, focusing on institutional capacity-strengthening and ensuring that victims participate meaningfully in decision-making. This approach enhances the effectiveness of individual and collective reparation measures, while ensuring that policies reflect the realities of affected communities. In parallel, IOM provides technical assistance to the Ministry of Interior and local governments to translate national frameworks into practical actions at the territorial level, adapted to diverse and often fragile contexts.
Aligned with the UN Secretary-General’s Action Agenda on Internal Displacement, IOM collaborates with the Department of National Planning (DNP), the Department for Social Prosperity (DPS), and the Victims’ Unit (UARIV) in advancing Colombia’s Durable Solutions Strategy. IOM leads the territorialization component, guiding seven prioritized cities in the formulation of participatory Durable Solutions Plans. These plans aim to reduce vulnerabilities of IDPs, strengthen social cohesion, and promote sustainable local integration by aligning institutional, community, and donor efforts.
Within the transitional justice framework established under the 2016 Peace Agreement, IOM provides technical and operational support to the Comprehensive System of Truth, Justice, Reparation, and Non-Repetition (SIVJRNR). In partnership with the Special Jurisdiction for Peace (JEP), IOM contributes to macro-case investigations, restorative projects, and victim participation. With the Missing Persons Search Unit (UBPD), IOM has advanced the implementation of Regional Search Plans, supporting the recovery, identification, and dignified restitution of remains in departments such as Antioquia, Huila, Caquetá, and Bolívar. At the same time, IOM supports the Agency for Reincorporation and Normalization (ARN) in advancing the sustainable reintegration of former combatants, facilitating income-generating initiatives and promoting participation in restorative justice processes.

This partnership promotes inclusive risk management systems by integrating gender, human mobility, and displacement considerations into national frameworks. Through this collaboration, IOM and UNGRD jointly advance technical assistance, information sharing, and policy alignment to ensure that Colombia’s preparedness and response systems are equitable, resilient, and people-centred.
At the national level, IOM maintains close coordination with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, DNP, and the Presidential Agency for International Cooperation (APC-Colombia) to ensure that international cooperation resources are strategically aligned with Colombia’s crisis response and recovery priorities. The IOM Country Strategy 2025–2028 reflects this alignment by integrating Colombia’s National Development Plan (2022–2026) , the UNSDCF, Global Compact for Migration (GCM), and IOM’s Strategic Results Framework (SRF). The strategy establishes a comprehensive roadmap for fulfilling the Organization’s mandate in Colombia, encompassing humanitarian response, emergency preparedness and institutional capacity-strengthening, peacebuilding, and the protection and integration of migrants and displaced populations. This integrated approach positions IOM as a key partner of the Government of Colombia in addressing the multifaceted challenges of displacement, migration, and crisis response, while contributing to long-term stability and sustainable development.

Aura and Sindy lead community actions to build climate resilience in La Guajira, Colombia © IOM 2026/Joaquín Aponte

The Indigenous Women Weaving Change

In La Guajira, Aura and Sindy are strengthening community preparedness and disaster management in a neighbourhood where the Ranchería River floods up to 80 per cent of homes during heavy rains. With IOM support, these indigenous leaders work with their community to map risks, identify safe shelters, contact authorities, and organize clean-up and recycling activities that reduce flood risk. Today, a network of nearly 50 community members has joined Aura and Sindy to respond to the challenges their neighbourhood faces, building a more resilient future. IOM advances safe, orderly, and regular migration in Colombia amid one of the world’s largest migration crises, with mixed flows intensified by conflict, disasters, and environmental degradation. The objective ensures mobility is managed responsibly while meeting protection and integration needs in crisis-affected, high-mobility areas.


Outcome 3a – Channels for regular migration are sustainable and responsive to current and emerging trends.

IOM supports the Government of Colombia to expand regularization as an entry point to social protection and basic services, reducing vulnerabilities for migrants and returnees and enabling inclusive pathways to socioeconomic integration.


Outcome 3b – Migration flows and cross-border mobility are well managed, with measures to ensure well-being throughout the mobility continuum.

In territories facing overlapping impacts of conflict and migration, IOM strengthens protection, health, and MHPSS systems — prioritizing prevention and assistance for trafficking in persons and migrant smuggling (including victims within the armed-conflict context) — to enhance safety, reduce risks, and uphold dignity along routes and at destination.


Outcome 3c – Migration policy and legal frameworks are coordinated through a whole-of-government approach and aligned with international standards.

IOM provides technical assistance to national and local authorities to strengthen migration governance, integrate mobility into territorial planning, and promote labour inclusion through public–private partnerships and evidence-based coordination mechanisms.


Outcome 3d – Migrants of all genders, ages, abilities and legal status are accepted into society and protected from violence, exploitation and abuse.

IOM fosters inclusion and protection through livelihoods and entrepreneurship support, community-based planning (CBP), leadership development, GBV-sensitive services in integration settings, and anti-xenophobia communication—so migrants can exercise their rights, contribute locally, and live free from violence and exploitation.

This approach informs efforts to prevent harm, ensure meaningful access to assistance, foster community participation and empowerment, and strengthen accountability to affected populations (AAP).
Equality, diversity, and inclusion are core pillars of IOM’s programming. In all sectors, IOM ensures equitable access to services for marginalized groups, including women, people with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression and sex characteristics (SOGIESC), children, youth, persons with disabilities, and ethnic minorities. Child protection is mainstreamed across to ensure that children are safeguarded from violence, exploitation, and harm. In alignment with IOM’s Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises , GBV risk mitigation is a shared responsibility. Each sector is required to assess and address potential GBV risks that may be exacerbated by their interventions, implementing effective measures to prevent and reduce these risks.
Reflecting its commitment to inclusive programming, community consultations are central to IOM’s interventions. In line with the “Leaving no one behind” principle, IOM actively engages local stakeholders in shaping and adapting interventions to meet the specific needs and vulnerabilities of each community. Feedback mechanisms —including suggestion boxes, focus group discussions, and a QR codes —enable beneficiaries to raise concerns and influence programming. These mechanisms reinforce AAP by promoting transparency, accountability, and trust between IOM and the communities it serves.
Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) is a cross-cutting priority. The missionIOM integrates risk mitigation measures into all interventions and ensures staff and partners are trained and supported to uphold PSEAH standards. Confidential and accessible reporting mechanisms are in place, and key messages on rights and conduct standards are widely disseminated to raise awareness. IOM works to ensure survivors of SEA receive timely, appropriate and survivor-centred support services. IOM also collaborates with the inter-agency PSEA Task Force to reinforce collective accountability and strengthen safer programming.

IOM leverages its expertise in data collection and analysis to provide actionable evidence for humanitarian and migration response in Colombia. Through the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and other innovative tools, IOM ensures timely, reliable, and protection-sensitive data on mobility flows, profiles, and needs of displaced populations, migrants, returnees, and host communities. Planned actions include:



Innovative Data Collection with DTM Wi-Fi Technology: Combining detection systems with the provision of internet connectivity at key mobility points. This initiative will produce analytical products, including trend reports, population profiles, interactive dashboards, validated datasets, and thematic bulletins, supporting evidence-based humanitarian planning.

Traditional Field-based DTM Monitoring: Ensuring continued presence and direct engagement at border crossings and other key locations. Protection and Privacy of Data: Ensuring compliance with ethical standards and safeguarding the rights and dignity of affected populations.



By combining innovation, traditional monitoring, and strong institutional partnerships, IOM ensures data-driven humanitarian response, anticipatory action, and forward-looking insights that contribute to better crisis management and durable solutions.

IOM Colombia will implement a multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) programme to provide rapid, flexible, and dignified support to families and individuals affected by conflict and disaster-related events. Designed to reduce vulnerability and enable beneficiaries to meet their most urgent needs, MPCA ensures crisis-affected households can access goods and services of their choice, while reinforcing coping strategies and resilience.


Planned interventions include:



Short-term Cash Transfers: Provision of multipurpose cash assistance for up to three months to households experiencing severe deprivation due to displacement, conflict, or climate-related emergencies. Transfers will be aligned with the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) developed by the Cash Working Group in Colombia.

Single Disbursement Support: Delivery of one-off cash transfers to migrants and refugees to address immediate basic needs, including food, hygiene items, and essential services.

Institutional Coordination: Implementation in close collaboration with the Unit for Attention and Reparation of Victims (UARIV) and the National Unit for Disaster Risk Management (UNGRD) to ensure complementarity with government-led responses and maximize institutional impact.

Integration with Broader Response: MPCA will be strategically linked with protection, shelter, and livelihoods interventions, creating pathways toward durable solutions and strengthening connections with national social protection systems.

IOM will put in place measures to ensuring safe, transparent, and accountable processes in multipurpose cash assistance—reinforcing safeguards with partners, service providers, and communities to protect beneficiaries from risks of SEA.



This intervention allows affected households to prioritize their own needs, promotes dignity and agency, and enhances coordination between humanitarian and institutional responses in Colombia.

IOM is a key CCCM actor in Colombia and will keep supporting displaced populations in collective centres, as well as those actors responding in those settings, through the following modalities:



Capacity Strengthening in the Management of Temporary Collective Centres: IOM will enhance the ability of local governments, communities, and key actors to manage temporary collective centres, ensuring they are better prepared for crises. This will be achieved through targeted training on site coordination and management, as well as cross-cutting themes such as protection, PSEAH, gender, localization and inclusion.

Strengthening Local Response Capacity: Through workshops, meetings, and training sessions, the cluster will strengthen the response capacity of communities, local governments, and local organizations—including women-led organizations (WLOs) and organizations of persons with disabilities (OPDs)—and other key actors. This will improve their ability to coordinate and manage temporary collective centres during displacement crises, ensuring more effective and inclusive service delivery. This will go in line with the humanitarian reset process and the flagship initiative piloted in Colombia.

Supporting the Meaningful Participation of Affected Populations: The cluster and sector will work to ensure the needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), victims of armed conflict, and those affected by natural hazards are met, as well as those of migrants and refugees. This will be achieved through the promotion and support of safe and inclusive site governance structures and mechanisms within collective centres, promoting participation and ensuring that displaced populations have access to relevant, safe and responsive two-way communication channels, including for sensitive complaints. IOM will also train community leaders on PSEAH and raise awareness among beneficiaries.

Expanding the Reach of CCCM Partners: As cluster lead, IOM will promote and facilitate the expansion of Cluster partners to additional geographical areas, enhancing the reach and impact of humanitarian assistance. This will enable more effective support for displaced populations living in collective centres across the country, while also reinforcing partners’ PSEAH capacity to ensure that site management is safe, accountable, and protective against risks of exploitation and abuse.

Strategic Engagement and Advocacy: IOM will foster strategic discussions with national and local authorities, relevant partners, and potential donors to position CCCM as a critical component of crisis response. These conversations will focus on raising awareness, covering the information gap in this regard, securing resources, and promoting policies that support durable solutions for displaced populations.

In Colombia’s complex crisis context, where displacement, migration, and armed violence intersect, protection needs are acute and multidimensional. IOM prioritizes safeguarding the rights and dignity of refugees, migrants, returnees, and host communities, while strengthening institutional and community systems to prevent risks, respond to violence and exploitation, and foster safe, inclusive environments.


This will be carried out through:



Protection and Assistance for Vulnerable Populations in Colombia: IOM strengthens local and national capacities to prevent protection risks for Venezuelan refugees, migrants, and host communities. This includes building community mechanisms to mitigate violence, exploitation, abuse, GBV, and neglect, and activating territorial assistance and referral pathways.

Comprehensive Protection Services: Through Reference and Orientation Points (PRO), IOM offers safe spaces for information, guidance, and referral to state protection services and humanitarian aid. In crisis contexts, such as displacement due to natural hazards, IOM provides individual protection assistance (IPA) via a one-stop shop model. Services also address housing, land, and property (HLP) issues, prioritizing individuals with specific protection needs through case management.

Quality GBV/SEA Response: IOM enhances GBV response, including survivors of SEA, capacities of territorial institutions by supplying specialized materials, training staff, and updating referral pathways.

Child Protection Processes: These efforts include monitoring, family reunification for unaccompanied or separated children, and strategies to prevent recruitment and exploitation of refugee and migrant children and youth.

Comprehensive Support for Trafficking Victims: Support for trafficking victims includes hygiene and protection kits, and comprehensive services for identification and assistance, applying an intersectional approach nationwide. A shelter in Bogotá D.C. supports territorial outreach.

Communication Strategy for Awareness and Reporting: In border areas, IOM promotes the LibertApp tool to identify and report human trafficking via alternative communication channels. Awareness campaigns will address trafficking, migrant smuggling, and guidance on activating protection pathways for extra-continental and intra-regional migrants, including women and lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, and intersex (LGBTIQ+) individuals. A prevention and response document will support these efforts.

Supporting Return and Socioeconomic Reintegration: IOM facilitates the implementation of the Colombian Law of Return (Law 1565/2012) by promoting awareness, supporting registration in the National Returnee Registry, and advancing productive reintegration through the Productive Return Accompaniment Plan (PARP). Service fairs connect returnees, migrants, and host communities with protection, health, education, and documentation services.

Productive Return Projects: Supporting Colombian returnees under Law 1565 of 2012 with training and advisory services on business planning, financial literacy, marketing, and technical skills to strengthen or establish productive ventures, ensuring sustainability and reintegration.

Community Engagement and Protection: To prevent trafficking for sexual exploitation, particularly in virtual environments, IOM provides grants to civil society organizations and transfers the “Let’s Navigate Safely” toolkit to government partners. Awareness-raising activities target children, adolescents, and community leaders to promote safe practices and reduce risks.

Institutional Strengthening for Anti-Trafficking Coordination: IOM strengthens the Interinstitutional Committee on Trafficking in Persons to advance the implementation of the National Anti-Trafficking Strategy, enhancing coordination among member entities and promoting effective mechanisms for prevention, victim identification, and comprehensive assistance.

IOM’s peacebuilding efforts in Colombia aim to strengthen resilience, prevent violence, and advance durable solutions for displacement-affected populations. By addressing conflict drivers and reinforcing community cohesion, these interventions foster environments conducive to peace, recovery, and long-term stability. IOM recognizes that mobility is both a coping and adaptation strategy in the face of crises and seeks to ensure that migration remains a choice, not a necessity. In Colombia, IOM works to strengthen preparedness, reduce disaster risks, and build resilience to climate change and conflict-related hazards, fully aligned with the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction and the Migrants in Countries in Crisis Guidelines .


To achieve this, IOM will focus on:



Strengthening Preparedness and Resilience of Vulnerable Communities in disaster- and conflict-prone areas, enhancing their capacity to anticipate, respond, and adapt to natural, socio-natural, and anthropogenic hazards.

Support to Governments at National and Local Levels in developing displacement-sensitive and gender-responsive disaster preparedness systems, including contingency planning, early warning mechanisms, and community risk management strategies.

Promotion of Regional Cooperation by implementing subregional initiatives Latin America that integrate gender and displacement considerations into disaster risk management policies.

Fostering Community-led Climate Adaptation, including the conservation and restoration of mangrove ecosystems on the Caribbean and Pacific coasts, through IOM’s community-based planning methodology. Activities include training on mangrove reforestation, emergency preparedness, and community committee formation, coupled with awareness and clean-up campaigns.

Advancing Ecosystem-based Solutions that reduce vulnerability, protect biodiversity, and strengthen disaster preparedness.

Supporting the Interinstitutional Roundtable on Climate and Environmental Displacement, led by the Ombudsman’s Office with UNGRD, DNP, and the Ministry of Environment, which seeks to formulate Colombia’s national policy on displacement caused by environmental and climate factors. Through this engagement, IOM provides strategic and technical assistance to integrate human mobility, protection, and inclusion perspectives into policy and planning processes, while strengthening local and community capacities. This includes training community defenders and institutions to strengthen preparedness, promote coordinated action, and safeguard the rights and resilience of populations affected by environmental degradation and disasters.



Through these integrated efforts, IOM contributes to reducing preventable losses, strengthening institutional and community resilience, and ensuring that displaced, migrant, and host populations are fully included in disaster risk management and climate adaptation strategies.

Ensuring safe, dignified, and voluntary mobility is a cornerstone of IOM’s crisis response in Colombia. Movement assistance is provided to reduce risks, safeguard dignity, and facilitate access to essential services for people affected by displacement, violence, and disasters. Interventions are designed in close coordination with government counterparts to guarantee protection-sensitive and timely support.


Key areas of focus are:



Emergency Transport in Crises: Providing safe movement assistance during displacement caused by violence, natural hazard-induced disasters, or other emergencies, using specialized vehicles or tickets for land transport to reduce exposure to risks and vulnerabilities.

Short-distance Transportation: Facilitating humanitarian transport within cities and municipalities to improve access for migrants to basic services such as medical care, education, documentation, and protection assistance.

Long-distance Relocation Support: Covering transport costs through ticket provision with certified transport companies, enabling migrants to safely relocate to destinations where they can access humanitarian assistance, family networks, or integration opportunities.

Integrated Coordination: Working alongside government institutions and local authorities to ensure that all movement assistance is complementary, protection-focused, and aligned with institutional responses.



By combining immediate lifesaving transport support with broader protection and integration objectives, IOM helps displaced and migrant populations move safely to locations where they can exercise their rights, access services, and begin pathways toward durable solutions.

Ensuring access to safe and regular migration pathways is critical in the Colombian context, where displacement, migration, and recurrent crises converge. IOM promotes solutions that prevent exploitation and irregular practices, with a focus on institutional strengthening, protection, and integration of migrants and returnees.


IOM will focus on:



Supporting Regularization Efforts: IOM provides technical and operational assistance to advance regularization processes, ensuring protection and access to services for migrants with irregular status. At the national level, IOM works with authorities to develop legal and policy frameworks that address remaining gaps in regularization. At the local level, IOM strengthens capacities of municipal and departmental governments to implement these measures effectively.

Strengthening Institutional Frameworks: IOM supports the Government of Colombia in implementing the National Strategy to Combat Migrant Smuggling, providing technical assistance to the Intersectoral Commission and developing territorial action plans. Capacity-strengthening efforts include training and equipping border management officials, improving technological systems, and enhancing coordination across migration and border agencies. These efforts also include improving the detection of fraudulent documents and identifying imposters, thereby reinforcing the integrity of border control operations.

Labour and Financial Inclusion: Through partnerships with the private and public sectors, IOM raises awareness on labour inclusion and supports job placement opportunities for migrants and returnees, complemented by technical assistance and targeted financial support to reduce barriers to formal employment.



Together, these interventions strengthen Colombia’s migration governance, protect vulnerable populations, and expand safe pathways, linking crisis response with longer-term integration and stability.

IOM plays a strategic role in strengthening humanitarian coordination in Colombia, particularly through its co-leadership of the Interagency Group on Mixed Migration Flows (GIFMM), alongside UNHCR. This role ensures that humanitarian action is coherent, effective, and responsive to the needs of refugees, migrants, returnees, and host communities, while also reinforcing the humanitarian reset and territorial coordination structures.


To achieve this, IOM will:



Consolidating Humanitarian Coordination: By supporting the humanitarian reset process in collaboration with OCHA, strengthening territorial teams, facilitating joint planning, and promoting engagement with local authorities.

Strengthening Inter-agency Capacities: Through sectoral trainings for GIFMM partners (up to five per sector annually), covering topics such as localization and post-distribution monitoring.

Enhancement of Information Management and Joint Analysis: Iincluding coordination of the Joint Needs Assessment (JNA), producing analytical reports, dashboards, and bulletins to guide evidence-based decision-making.

Improvement of Communication with Affected Populations: By maintaining and upgrading digital platforms such as the GIFMM Service Mapping, the “GIFMM Contigo” app, and a new chatbot to provide reliable, real-time information and collect community feedback.

Support to Strategic Dialogue and Advocacy: By organizing GIFMM plenary sessions, regional coordination meetings, and donor conferences, as well as preparing annual and periodic reports that highlight achievements, funding gaps, and emerging trends.

Raising Awareness on Migration Dynamics: Through targeted campaigns in high-mobility areas such as Necoclí and Juradó, helping to inform and guide migrants on available services and opportunities. Through these efforts, IOM consolidates its leadership in humanitarian coordination, enhances the quality of response, and ensures strategic alignment with Colombia’s humanitarian architecture.
