0 U.S. nonprofits and Tribes building research capacity in maternity care deserts Non-Profit & Social Impact Non-Profit & Social Impact Connecting the Community for Maternal Health Challenge: Research Capacity Building in Maternity Care Deserts (CCMH 2. 0) Maternal mortality remains a serious public health challenge in the United States, with rural and under-resourced communities facing disproportionately high rates.
Many of these communities are located in "maternity care deserts," areas lacking hospitals, birth centers, or obstetric providers, and community-based organizations working in these areas often lack the research infrastructure needed to fully participate in and lead maternal health research. Building on the success of the original 2022 Connecting the Community for Maternal Health Challenge , CCMH 2.
0 is a National Institutes of Health (NIH) prize competition under the IMPROVE initiative that rewards non-academic 501(c)(3) organizations, advocacy groups, and state or federally recognized Tribes for building the research capacity and infrastructure needed to lead or partner in maternal health research, with special emphasis on maternity care deserts and, newly, research into fathers&#x27; role in maternal health outcomes.
Through this multi-phase competition, participating organizations will strengthen their ability to lead or partner in future NIH- and HHS-funded maternal health research, ensuring the communities most affected by poor maternal health outcomes have a central role in shaping the research that serves them.
The Challenge is open to U.S.-based non-academic 501(c)(3) organizations and state or federally recognized Tribes; academic partnerships are welcomed but not required. Maternal mortality in the U.S. remains high relative to peer nations, with 649 women dying of maternal causes in 2024 (a rate of 17. 9 deaths per 100,000 live births).
Over 5. 5 million U.S. women live in areas with no or limited access to obstetric care, and more than 35% of U.S. counties are considered maternity care deserts, meaning they have no hospitals, birth centers, or obstetric providers. Rural maternal mortality rates run as much as 65% higher than urban rates, and closures of rural obstetric units are likely to widen this gap further.
In 2019, NIH launched the IMPROVE initiative to reduce preventable maternal deaths and improve outcomes before, during, and after delivery, with community engagement as a core priority. The Recruitment Phase is 6 weeks long and is open to all community-based or advocacy organizations and Tribes that meet the eligibility criteria stated below in the Rules section of this announcement.
Informational webinars during this period will introduce the challenge sponsors at NICHD, provide general guidance on proposal submission, and share the opportunities that participation in this challenge will provide. There will be question and answer sessions at the end of every webinar.
We recognize that many organizations may be unfamiliar with the process of working with the NIH and with developing and articulating their research ideas. Introductory materials will be provided during the Recruitment Phase to orient them to the research process and to the Challenge process.
At the end of the Recruitment Phase, organizations that are interested in continuing in this Challenge will be asked to complete a very short Training Phase Entry Application to participate in the Training Phase. Organizations do not need to participate in the Recruitment Phase to submit a Training Phase Entry Application.
Training Phase Entry Applications will be evaluated based on the Training Phase Entry Application Evaluation Criteria stated below. Up to 50 organizations will be accepted to participate in the Training Phase. Up to 50 Training Phase participants will receive deeper and more customized training for six weeks.
Training services will include online training and other forms of educational guidance to help organizations create a complete and competitive Training Phase Final Submission that organizations must complete to be considered for participation in the Proposal Phase of the competition.
The Training Phase participants will have access to training webinars, join weekly workshops, and receive feedback on one draft of their Training Phase Final Submission. The Training Phase Final Submission will consist of a brief, one-page proposal describing their organization and the research idea they wish to implement if selected to participate in the later phases of the challenge.
Research ideas must be relevant to maternal health, address community-identified needs, and specifically align with the objectives of the IMPROVE initiative (see specific requirements in evaluation/judging criteria below). Only organizations that participated in the Training Phase can submit a proposal to advance to the next phase of the Challenge, the Proposal Phase.
Up to 20 organizations will be selected to advance to the Proposal Phase of the challenge based upon the Training Phase Final Submission Judging Criteria described below. Each of the 20 advancing organizations will receive a $10,000 award. In this third phase, each of the 20 participating organizations selected to advance from the Training Phase will develop a comprehensive research proposal over the course of 10 weeks.
Each organization will be custom matched with a mentor and will be invited to participate in regular workshops and webinars on relevant topics. Interactions with mentor(s) and one another will help each organization develop a fully realized research proposal that is well-aligned with the objectives of the IMPROVE initiative .
For many non-profit organizations with limited resources, this kind of sustained effort is extremely difficult to undertake. The $10,000 prizes awarded in the Training Phase could be used to help support their continued participation in this phase.
Up to 10 organizations will be selected to advance to the Research Phase based on the Proposal Phase Judging Criteria and will receive a $150,000 award that could be used to stand up their research infrastructures and pursue their proposed research activities.
In this fourth and final phase, the 10 organizations selected to advance from the Proposal Phase will have approximately 18 months to implement their project plans, conduct the proposed research, and report results.
During this phase, each organization will continue to meet regularly with their respective mentors for specific advice and guidance, and all finalists will also be invited to participate in workshops and webinars to hear from different subject matter experts on topics related to maternal health and more broadly towards sustaining their research activities.
For the Research Phase, services of an Institutional Review Board will be provided if needed. At the end of this phase, each organization will present in a research showcase and later submit a report that summarizes their activities, research findings, and conclusions. This report will also include a project plan outlining the next steps resulting from the initial findings and a proposed supporting budget.
Organizations that are judged to have met the minimum criteria and satisfactorily provided the required information in their final research report will be eligible to receive one of up to eight 3 rd Place prizes of $100,000 each.
Those organizations who are judged to have exceeded the minimum criteria and demonstrated successful completion of their proposed research projects will be considered for the 1 st Place prize of $350,000 and the 2 nd Place prize of $250,000. Any prize purse funds that remain un-awarded following the completion of the Research Phase may be added to each of the prize amounts awarded to the winners of the Research Phase.
The 1 st Place, 2 nd Place, and up to eight 3 rd Place prizes will be awarded based on several factors, including reported outcomes from this phase and performance during the Research Phase. Those specific factors will be announced at the conclusion of the Proposal Phase and prior to the start of the Research Phase.
