## What is AI Startup Due Diligence?

AI due diligence is the structured process of assessing the risks, benefits, compliance, and ethical implications of artificial intelligence systems. This evaluation ensures that AI technologies align with organizational goals while adhering to legal and ethical standards. By systematically reviewing these aspects, businesses can identify opportunities for innovation and address potential vulnerabilities before deployment.

A well-executed AI due diligence process minimizes oversight errors, often reducing them by 20–30%. It also uncovers hidden risks and ensures that AI systems operate transparently and responsibly. This approach is critical for organizations aiming to integrate AI solutions effectively while maintaining trust and accountability.

### Technical Assessments

The technical evaluation focuses on the functionality, performance, and architecture of the AI system. This includes analyzing the scalability of the technology, its ability to handle real-world data, and the robustness of its algorithms. A deep dive into the system’s architecture can reveal potential bottlenecks or limitations that may impact future growth. Performance metrics, such as accuracy and processing speed, are also critical for understanding the system’s reliability in practical applications.

### Legal and Ethical Reviews

Legal and ethical considerations are equally important in AI due diligence. Ensuring regulatory compliance is essential, especially in industries with strict data privacy laws or ethical guidelines. Investors must assess whether the startup adheres to these regulations and addresses ethical challenges, such as bias in AI algorithms or transparency in decision-making processes. Neglecting these aspects can lead to reputational risks and legal complications down the line.

### Evaluating Team Expertise

The expertise of the startup’s team often determines the success of an AI venture. Investors should evaluate the team’s technical skills, industry knowledge, and ability to adapt to evolving challenges. A capable team not only drives innovation but also ensures the AI solution remains competitive in a rapidly changing market.

Understanding these components provides a comprehensive view of the startup’s potential and safeguards against unforeseen risks.

## A Step-by-Step AI Due Diligence Process

Conducting AI due diligence requires a structured approach to ensure no critical detail is overlooked. This step-by-step process provides a clear roadmap for startups and investors to evaluate AI systems comprehensively, from setting objectives to final reporting.

### 1. Define Clear Objectives

The first step is to establish the purpose and scope of the due diligence. Whether the goal is to assess technical feasibility, compliance, or scalability, defining specific objectives ensures the process remains focused and efficient. This clarity helps align the efforts of all stakeholders involved.

### 2. Assemble a Team of Specialists

AI systems are complex, requiring expertise across various domains. Bringing together specialists in data science, legal compliance, and industry-specific applications ensures that every aspect of the system is thoroughly evaluated. A multidisciplinary team can identify risks and opportunities that might otherwise go unnoticed.

### 3. Collect Accurate Data and Documentation

Gathering comprehensive and accurate data is the backbone of effective due diligence. This includes technical documentation, datasets, algorithmic details, and compliance records. Proper documentation enables a detailed analysis of the AI system’s functionality, ethical considerations, and potential vulnerabilities.

### 4. Evaluate Risks and Opportunities

With the data in hand, the next step is to assess risks and identify opportunities. This involves evaluating the AI system’s performance, scalability, and alignment with regulatory standards. Risk evaluation also includes analyzing potential biases in the data and ensuring the system adheres to ethical guidelines.

### 5. Prepare a Comprehensive Report

The final phase is to compile findings into a well-structured report. This document should summarize key insights, highlight risks, and provide actionable recommendations. A clear and detailed report supports informed decision-making, whether for investment, acquisition, or internal deployment.

## Challenges and Red Flags in AI Due Diligence

Startup due diligence often uncovers challenges unique to AI investments, especially when critical issues remain undetected.

One common challenge is incomplete data collection, which can obscure the true capabilities and limitations of an AI system. Without a comprehensive audit, investors risk overlooking technical gaps or operational inefficiencies that could derail future scalability.

Another significant concern is the presence of hidden biases within AI models. These biases, often embedded in training data, can lead to inaccurate predictions or discriminatory outcomes, jeopardizing both ethical compliance and market viability. Early identification of such biases is crucial to avoid costly remediation efforts later.

Operational vulnerabilities are rising. AI tools like ChatGPT, Copilot, and Claude incurred over 4.2 million data loss violations in 2025. These incidents underscore the urgency of addressing security and data governance early in due diligence.

Regulatory oversights also pose a substantial risk. With AI regulations evolving rapidly, failing to account for compliance requirements can result in legal penalties or reputational damage. For example, the acquisition of an AI startup by a Japanese conglomerate highlighted how thorough audits can uncover unseen technical gaps, ensuring alignment with market and regulatory expectations.

Regulatory risks have intensified. AI-driven phishing attacks rose 1,265% since late 2022, and credential theft increased 967%. This dramatic surge highlights the increasing need for proactive compliance and cybersecurity diligence in AI investments.

### Data Governance and Licensing Risks in AI Due Diligence

Expanding on operational challenges, data governance and licensing compliance present significant risks in AI due diligence. Unclear data ownership, improper licensing, or non-compliance with privacy regulations can jeopardize deals and expose organizations to legal liabilities. Proactively auditing data provenance and securing proper rights are essential steps to mitigate these risks. Addressing these issues early helps ensure a defensible and compliant due diligence process.

## Specialized KPIs and Metrics to Analyze

Evaluating AI performance requires a focus on metrics that go beyond surface-level indicators. Model accuracy stands as a cornerstone, ensuring the AI system delivers reliable predictions or outputs. Equally important is data quality , which directly impacts the model’s ability to perform effectively. Poor-quality data can lead to skewed results, undermining the system’s reliability.

Scalability is another critical metric, as it determines whether the AI solution can handle increased workloads or adapt to growing demands without compromising performance. User adoption rates also offer valuable insights, highlighting how well the AI integrates into workflows and meets user expectations. Finally, cost efficiency rounds out the evaluation, helping stakeholders assess whether the AI delivers value proportional to its investment.

By focusing on these specialized KPIs, investors can pinpoint the metrics that truly matter, ensuring their assessments align with long-term success.

Compliance metrics cannot be ignored. EU AI Act fines reach up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover for prohibited AI practices. Companies must integrate robust compliance monitoring into KPI frameworks to avoid severe penalties.

## Where the AI Lives

The backbone of artificial intelligence lies in its hosting environment, which plays a pivotal role in ensuring reliability and performance. Organizations often face the decision between cloud-based solutions and on-premises infrastructure, each offering distinct advantages and challenges.

Cloud hosting provides unparalleled scalability, allowing AI systems to adapt to fluctuating demands without significant upfront investment. This flexibility is particularly beneficial for businesses aiming to expand their AI capabilities rapidly. On the other hand, on-premises solutions offer greater control and security, making them ideal for industries handling sensitive data or requiring compliance with strict regulations.

Cost considerations also differ significantly. While cloud hosting operates on a subscription model, reducing initial expenses, on-premises setups involve higher upfront costs but may prove more economical in the long term for large-scale operations.

Understanding these hosting environments is crucial for building reliable AI systems that align with organizational goals and priorities.

## Who Actually Owns the AI Part?

Ownership of AI solutions often involves a tangled web of intellectual property (IP) rights, licensing agreements, and data usage policies. When multiple stakeholders contribute to an AI project—such as developers, data providers, and business partners—determining who owns what can become highly complex. For instance, third-party data or open-source tools integrated into the solution may carry specific licensing terms that affect ownership rights.

To avoid disputes, clear documentation of IP ownership is essential from the outset. Contracts should explicitly outline who retains rights to the AI model, its outputs, and any proprietary data used in training. Additionally, businesses must consider the implications of partnership agreements, which may grant shared or limited rights to the AI’s intellectual property. By addressing these details early, organizations can ensure smoother collaboration and protect their investments in AI innovation.
