## CAIDRA Section 15.3 PCCP Lesson

15.3.1 • Scope before mechanics
## What a PCCP Actually Is in 2026

A Predetermined Change Control Plan is not simply an internal “SOP for learning,” and it is not a new stand-alone device pathway. It is a plan reviewed and authorized as part of an existing FDA device marketing authorization so that specified future modifications can be implemented without a separate marketing submission for each covered change.

Statute
#### FD&C Act § 515C
Added by FDORA in 2022, section 515C expressly provides for PCCPs for devices. For PMA and 510(k) devices, a change that would otherwise require a supplement or new 510(k) can avoid that additional premarket filing when it is consistent with an FDA-authorized PCCP.

Current final guidance
#### FDA PCCP guidance — August 18, 2025
The current document was originally issued December 4, 2024 and reissued August 18, 2025. It gives nonbinding recommendations for PCCPs tailored to AI-enabled device software functions (AI-DSFs).

Regulation
#### QMSR — effective February 2, 2026
The revised 21 CFR Part 820 Quality Management System Regulation incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference. PCCP implementation must live inside the manufacturer’s applicable quality system—not in a parallel “AI exception” process.

Separate draft
#### AI-enabled device lifecycle guidance — January 2025
FDA’s broader AI-enabled-device lifecycle and marketing-submission guidance remains draft and not for implementation. Do not blur its proposed recommendations into the final PCCP guidance.

#### What the current PCCP guidance covers
AI-enabled device software functions intended to be modified over time.
AI-DSFs reviewed through 510(k), De Novo, or PMA pathways.
The device constituent part of a device-led combination product when that constituent includes an AI-DSF.
Manually implemented changes, automatically implemented changes, or a combination.
Changes that generally would otherwise require an additional device marketing submission.

#### What it does not automatically cover
The drug or biologic constituent part of a device-led combination product.
A generic “PCCP for drugs” or “PCCP for pharmacovigilance.”
Every routine software change that would not otherwise require a marketing submission.
Any change merely because it is technically feasible or described in an internal SOP.
A modification that was not authorized in the PCCP or was not implemented according to the authorized Modification Protocol.

#### Regulatory nuance: “pre-authorized” does not mean “unreviewed”
The regulatory work happens prospectively. FDA evaluates the future modification boundaries, verification and validation methods, acceptance criteria, impact assessment, update process, and related controls when it reviews the PCCP. A covered change can later be implemented without another marketing submission because that change-control framework has already been reviewed.

2019 FDA discussion paper introduces a proposed framework for modifications to AI/ML-based SaMD.

2022 FDORA adds section 515C, giving FDA express statutory authority for device PCCPs.

2023 FDA issues draft AI-device PCCP guidance; FDA, Health Canada, and MHRA publish five PCCP guiding principles.

2024 → 2025 Final guidance is originally issued Dec. 2024 and reissued as the current version Aug. 18, 2025.

2026 QMSR is effective. Device change-control records and implementation now sit within the updated Part 820 quality framework.

#### Key takeaway
Think of a PCCP as prospectively authorized device change control . The internal SOPs, MLOps controls, risk-management file, validation procedures, labeling workflow, and quality records support it—but none of those artifacts alone is the PCCP.

15.3.2 • Official structure
## Deconstructing a PCCP Without Inventing a New Taxonomy

The final guidance describes three official PCCP components. CAIDRA can use memorable teaching language, but the official titles matter in regulatory work.

Figure 1. The three official PCCP components

Official component 1
#### Description of Modifications
Identifies the specific planned modifications and the resulting characteristics or performance changes that the manufacturer intends to implement.

Official component 2
#### Modification Protocol
Defines how each modification will be developed, verified, validated, evaluated against predefined acceptance criteria, implemented, and communicated.

Official component 3
#### Impact Assessment
Assesses the benefits and risks of the planned modifications and connects those risks to appropriate mitigations and controls.

The official framework is “Description of Modifications + Modification Protocol + Impact Assessment.” Terms such as “sandbox,” “recipe,” or “proof” can help learners remember the concepts, but they are CAIDRA teaching language—not FDA component names.

CAIDRA teaching lens
#### WHAT → HOW → IMPACT
WHAT can change? HOW will each authorized change be developed and validated? IMPACT : why does the planned change remain acceptable when benefits, risks, cumulative effects, mitigations, and user impact are considered? This is useful shorthand, but it should never replace the official component names in a submission.

### The Modification Protocol Has Four Primary Content Areas

1
Data management Collection, annotation/reference standard, curation, storage, retention, quality assurance, representativeness, and train/tune/test separation.

2
Re-training practices Objectives, model components subject to change, training methods, and—if proposed—specific architecture or hyperparameter modifications with rationale.

3
Performance evaluation Verification and validation activities, datasets, statistical analysis, clinically appropriate metrics, and pre-defined acceptance criteria.

4
Update procedures Release process, labeling and communication, deployment method, versioning, rollback/stop controls, transparency, and real-world monitoring as applicable.

#### What this does NOT mean
A PCCP is not a blanket permission to “continuously learn.” It can describe updates that occur continuously, periodically, or rarely. It can involve automatic implementation, manual implementation, or both. The authorization is defined by the specific modifications and protocol that FDA reviewed—not by the buzzword used for the model.

15.3.3 • Component 1
## Writing the Description of Modifications: Specific, Bounded, Verifiable

FDA recommends a limited number of modifications that are specific and can be verified and validated. Each planned modification should be linked to the corresponding performance-evaluation activity in the Modification Protocol.

Description of Modifications design questions Question What a defensible PCCP should make clear Common overstatement to avoid

What is changing? Enumerate each planned modification and describe the resulting change in device characteristics, performance, inputs, compatibility, use, or other authorized characteristics. “The model may improve over time.” That is too vague to evaluate prospectively.

How will it be implemented? State whether implementation is automatic, manual, or hybrid. If automatic, define boundaries/guardrails that constrain the update range. Assuming FDA only allows human-reviewed/manual promotions. The current final guidance explicitly says FDA will consider automatically implemented modifications.

Where will it apply? Clarify whether changes are global/homogeneous or local/heterogeneous, and identify the factors that warrant local adaptation. Assuming every deployed device must receive an identical model version.

How often? Describe expected update frequency—from infrequent changes in a largely locked device to continuous adaptation, when appropriate. Inventing a universal quarterly or annual retraining cadence.

What remains within scope? Modifications must remain within the device’s intended use. In general they should remain within indications for use, although FDA notes that certain indications changes may be appropriate and strongly encourages Q-Submission discussion. “Any indication change is always forbidden.” The final guidance is more nuanced.

Can architecture change? Do not assume the architecture must always remain fixed. If re-training includes specific architecture modifications, FDA recommends describing and justifying them in the Modification Protocol; whether they are appropriate depends on the specific PCCP and device. “PCCPs can only change model weights, never architecture.”

### Types of Changes FDA Identifies as Potentially Appropriate

#### Performance specifications
For example, performance improvements from re-training on new data within the intended-use population and same type/range of input signal.

#### Inputs and compatibility
For example, new sources of the same input type, compatible hardware/software versions, operating-system changes, cloud infrastructure changes, or limited new input types when appropriately specified.

#### Certain use/performance changes
For example, a specific subpopulation within the originally indicated population may be supportable when the plan and evidence are appropriate.

#### Device-specific boundaries
A modification that is appropriate for one device may not be appropriate for another. Benefit-risk profile, device function, proposed change, and FDA experience matter.

#### Director’s question: “Is this change truly in the authorized PCCP?”
Do not answer from a generic eligibility table. Ask two concrete questions: (1) Was this exact type of modification specified in the authorized Description of Modifications? (2) Can it be implemented exactly according to the authorized Modification Protocol? If either answer is no, move to the device-modification assessment and FDA interaction rather than assuming the PCCP covers it.

15.3.4 • Component 2
## The Modification Protocol: Generate the Evidence You Would Otherwise Need

The protocol is not merely a flowchart or generic SOP. It is the methods package that prospectively specifies how each planned modification will be developed, validated, implemented, and controlled while maintaining device safety and effectiveness.

Figure 2. A risk-based Modification Protocol workflow

1
Qualify the change & data Confirm the candidate modification is within the authorized Description and apply the approved data-management methods.

2
Develop/re-train Use the specified methods, version controls, model components, hyperparameters, and retraining procedures.

3
Evaluate against acceptance criteria Run the pre-specified analytical/clinical/statistical evaluation using independent test data and appropriate metrics.

4
Update, communicate & monitor Implement only after criteria are met; perform specified labeling/user communication, version control, monitoring, and rollback/stop actions.

The goal is not to mechanize every update identically. It is to define methods rigorous enough that the evidence generated for each covered change supports continued safety and effectiveness.

### 1. Data Management: Independence Is the Rule; a Frozen “Golden Set Forever” Is Not

FDA recommends clear control of training, tuning, and test data, including representative data, reference-standard procedures, quality assurance, sequestration, and safeguards against leakage. Test data should be independent of model development. The guidance also asks manufacturers to explain the relationship between data used for the original and subsequent AI-DSF versions.

#### Correcting a common shortcut
“Use one immutable golden test set for every future model forever” is not an FDA universal requirement and may itself create problems from repeated reuse, obsolescence, or mismatch to current practice. A PCCP should specify a scientifically defensible test-data strategy that preserves independence, relevance, representativeness, and control for the planned modifications.

### 2. Re-training: Pre-specify What Can Change

The re-training section should identify the processing steps subject to change and the methods used to implement the planned modifications. If the proposed re-training includes specific architecture changes—such as changes to training hyperparameters, nodes, or layers—the guidance recommends describing and justifying them. This is why “architecture is always locked” is too categorical.

### 3. Performance Evaluation: Acceptance Criteria Must Fit the Device and Change

Choose metrics and acceptance criteria that are clinically and scientifically justified for the specific function and consequence of error. Sensitivity, specificity, precision, negative/positive predictive values, AUROC, calibration, error rates, human-AI performance, robustness, subgroup performance, or other endpoints may matter depending on the device. No universal FDA rule says every update must pass the same non-inferiority margin or use one particular metric.

#### Illustrative—not regulatory—example
A radiology triage PCCP might pre-specify a sensitivity acceptance criterion because missed urgent findings are clinically important, while also constraining false-positive workload. The exact thresholds, confidence intervals, datasets, and statistical methods would need to be justified for that device. A “2% margin” is an example parameter, not an FDA default.

### 4. Update Procedures: Deployment Is More Than “Go Live”

The update procedure should address how a validated modification is implemented and how the manufacturer manages communication, labeling, versioning, user training where appropriate, rollback or stop mechanisms, cybersecurity and interoperability implications, and real-world monitoring as applicable. The authorized PCCP should make clear whether deployment is automatic, manual, or hybrid.

#### Key takeaway
The Modification Protocol should be capable of generating and retaining the information that would otherwise have supported a separate FDA submission for the covered modification. “MLOps automation” can implement parts of the process, but automation does not replace risk management, validation, traceability, or quality-system responsibilities.

15.3.5 • Component 3 + post-authorization
## Impact Assessment, Implementation, QMSR, and Reporting

The Impact Assessment connects the planned changes to benefit-risk reasoning and mitigation. After authorization, implementation must remain inside the authorized plan and the manufacturer’s quality system.

### Impact Assessment: More Than Monitoring

The formal Impact Assessment is primarily the prospective assessment of the benefits and risks associated with the planned modifications and the adequacy of mitigations. Ongoing monitoring can be an important control and is addressed within the Modification Protocol/update procedures as applicable, but it should not be taught as though “Impact Assessment” simply means “postmarket dashboard.”

Individual-change risk: What new hazards, failure modes, biases, or use errors could the modification introduce?

Cumulative risk: Could a sequence of individually acceptable changes alter behavior, user interaction, or performance in a way that matters?

Risk of not changing: Could performance, compatibility, or clinical relevance degrade if the device remains unchanged?

Mitigation linkage: Which protocol controls, acceptance criteria, labeling changes, monitoring actions, or rollback mechanisms address each identified risk?

Figure 3. The implementation decision after a PCCP is authorized

1
Compare to authorized Description Is this planned modification actually one FDA reviewed in the PCCP?

2
Follow the authorized Protocol Can the manufacturer execute the specified data, validation, acceptance, update, and communication methods?

3
Implement only if consistent If both conditions are met, the change can be implemented without a new marketing submission.

4
If not consistent, reassess Use applicable device-modification requirements/guidance; a significant change likely needs a new submission and FDA discussion may be appropriate.

“Not in the PCCP” does not automatically mean “new 510(k)” in every circumstance; it means the manufacturer must evaluate the change under the applicable statutory, regulatory, and device-modification framework. Conversely, a change listed in the PCCP but implemented outside the authorized protocol may still require a new submission.

### The 2026 Quality-System Baseline: QMSR

FDA’s older PCCP text contains QSR-era citations because the guidance was written before the February 2, 2026 effective date. For a 2026 lesson, the operative device quality baseline is the Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) , which amended Part 820 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference. Manufacturers must maintain appropriate design/change controls, records, risk-management interfaces, corrective-action processes, supplier controls, and other applicable QMS requirements for the device and its PCCP implementation.

#### Part 11 nuance: do not make every MLOps event a fictional universal Part 11 mandate
21 CFR Part 11 applies when electronic records/signatures fall within its scope—principally electronic records required by FDA predicate rules or electronic records submitted to FDA, as described in FDA’s Part 11 scope guidance. For PCCP workflows, trustworthy, attributable, time-ordered records and auditability are strong controls, but whether a particular event log or electronic signature is specifically a Part 11 record depends on the underlying recordkeeping requirement and how the organization relies on the electronic record.

### Reporting and Transparency: Avoid the “Annual Report for Every PCCP Change” Rule

PCCP implementation reporting distinctions Situation 2026 teaching point

Covered modification implemented per PCCP No new marketing submission is necessary for that modification. Document the modification and analysis in the quality system and execute the labeling/communication actions specified in the authorized PCCP.

PMA device Applicable PMA post-approval reporting requirements and conditions of approval still apply. Periodic reporting may be relevant; do not generalize PMA reporting rules to every 510(k) or De Novo device.

Public/user transparency FDA recommends describing the PCCP in public device summaries and updating appropriate labeling so users understand relevant implemented changes, performance, validation evidence, and how updates affect use.

Modification to the PCCP itself FDA states that changes to an authorized PCCP will generally require review in a new marketing submission for the modified device/PCCP. Q-Submission discussion is encouraged.

15.3.6 • Automatic and local adaptation
## Automatic Updates Are Possible—but Complexity Must Be Designed Into the PCCP

One of the most important changes from early educational shorthand is that FDA’s final guidance explicitly contemplates PCCPs for automatically implemented modifications. The manufacturer should clearly state whether updates are automatic, manual, or hybrid and, for automatic updates, establish appropriate boundaries or guardrails.

#### Periodic manual update
A manufacturer collects new data quarterly, develops a candidate model offline, performs the specified evaluation, obtains internal release approval, updates labeling as required, and deploys the new version.

#### Automatic implementation
Software automatically applies a modification when the pre-authorized data, method, acceptance, and guardrail conditions are met. This is not automatically prohibited, but FDA highlights additional complexity and recommends Q-Submission discussion.

#### Global update
All deployed devices receive the same validated modification under the authorized plan.

#### Local adaptation
Different devices/sites may implement different authorized changes based on specified local conditions. The PCCP should define the local factors and how users remain informed.

#### Do not equate “continuous learning” with uncontrolled online learning
A device can update frequently or even continuously only within the authorized change boundaries and protocol. The regulatory question is not whether the model “learns in real time”; it is whether the planned modification mechanism, evidence generation, acceptance criteria, risk controls, transparency, and quality-system execution support continued safety and effectiveness.

15.3.7 • Cross-center boundaries
## PCCP Beyond Device Regulation: Use the Principle Carefully

FDA’s medical-product centers collaborate on AI, but cross-center collaboration does not turn the device PCCP framework into a universal GxP regulatory pathway.

PCCP scope across FDA contexts Context Formal PCCP status What to do instead / additionally

AI-enabled device software function Formal FDA PCCP pathway available through applicable 510(k), De Novo, or PMA authorization. Use section 515C/current final guidance, device risk management, QMSR, and applicable submission guidance.

Device constituent of device-led combination product Potentially within guidance for the AI-DSF device constituent. Engage FDA early if the AI modification affects the drug/biologic constituent; the PCCP recommendations do not apply to that constituent part.

AI supporting drug/biologic regulatory decision-making No separate formal “PCCP for drugs” pathway established by this device guidance. Use the applicable drug/biologic regulatory framework, risk-based credibility evidence, change control, and FDA engagement. The January 2025 AI credibility guidance remains draft.

PV, clinical, or quality-system AI tool Do not call an internal SOP an FDA-authorized PCCP unless it actually is part of an authorized device PCCP. Maintain scientifically justified lifecycle/change controls under the applicable regulated process and electronic-record/quality requirements. A “PCCP-like” structure can be a CAIDRA governance heuristic, but label it as such.

AI in drug/biologic manufacturing Device PCCP guidance does not establish the manufacturing change-reporting pathway. Apply cGMP, validated process/system controls, regulatory reporting categories, product-specific commitments, and early FDA engagement appropriate to the manufacturing use.

CAIDRA governance heuristic
#### “PCCP-like” thinking can still be useful
For non-device AI, teams can borrow the disciplined questions—what may change, how change is qualified/validated, what risks it creates, who approves it, how it is monitored, and what triggers escalation. But the lesson must say explicitly that this is an internal governance pattern unless an applicable regulator has established a formal PCCP mechanism for that product/use.

15.3.8 • Worked application
## Worked PCCP Case: Retinal Image Triage Software

Assume FDA authorizes an AI-enabled device software function that triages retinal images for priority review by an ophthalmologist. The authorized PCCP includes several planned improvements while keeping the device within its intended use.

Worked PCCP modification decisions Proposed change How to analyze it Likely regulatory posture

Retrain using additional images from the same prespecified acquisition types and population to improve performance. Confirm this exact retraining change is in the Description; execute the authorized data-management, retraining, performance-evaluation, and update procedures. Can be implemented under the PCCP if all authorized conditions and acceptance criteria are met.

Add compatibility with an additional scanner model that was explicitly included in the PCCP. Use the corresponding compatibility/validation testing and any labeling updates specified in the protocol. Can be PCCP-covered if authorized and implemented in conformance with the plan.

Add a scanner that was never described in the authorized PCCP. The change is outside the authorized Description. Evaluate under applicable device-modification requirements and discuss with FDA if uncertainty remains. Do not assume automatic PCCP eligibility. A new submission may be required depending on significance.

Change from clinician triage support to autonomous diagnosis. This changes the intended use/function in a fundamental way and is outside the PCCP teaching scenario. Not a routine PCCP update. Requires a separate regulatory assessment and likely new marketing authorization.

Replace the model with a new architecture. Do not use a universal yes/no rule. Determine whether that specific architecture modification was actually proposed, justified, and authorized in the PCCP and whether the protocol can support it. Device- and PCCP-specific. If not authorized, evaluate as a new modification; do not assume it is permitted merely because performance is similar.

### Director’s Pre-Deployment Questions

1. Scope
Can I point to the exact authorized modification and trace it to the corresponding protocol activity?

2. Evidence
Did the candidate version meet every pre-specified, clinically justified acceptance criterion using controlled and independent evidence?

3. Risk
Did implementation create a new hazard, cumulative effect, subgroup problem, interoperability issue, or user-behavior risk not covered by the Impact Assessment?

4. Quality system
Are the change, approvals, design/development evidence, nonconformances, and corrective actions controlled under the QMSR and applicable procedures?

5. Transparency
Were labeling, version history, user communication, and training updated exactly as specified by the PCCP?

6. Escalation
If anything deviated from the authorized plan, did the team stop and perform the applicable regulatory assessment rather than forcing the change through the PCCP?

#### Section 15.3 durable playbook
Authorize the change space prospectively. Generate evidence exactly as promised. Implement only inside that authorized space. Treat deviations as regulatory events, not merely engineering exceptions. That is the core discipline behind a defensible PCCP.

Authoritative sources
## 2026 Reference Set and Regulatory Status

These references are included so learners can distinguish binding law/regulation from nonbinding FDA guidance and international guiding principles. The lesson does not ask learners to memorize citations.

#### FD&C Act § 515C — Predetermined Change Control Plans for Devices
Law
Express statutory authority added by FDORA. The FDA final guidance explains how this authority applies to AI-enabled device software functions.

#### FDA — Marketing Submission Recommendations for a PCCP for AI-Enabled Device Software Functions
Final • Aug 2025
Current final PCCP guidance; issued August 18, 2025 and originally issued December 4, 2024. Core source for Description of Modifications, Modification Protocol, Impact Assessment, implementation, labeling, and authorized-change policy.

#### FDA — AI-Enabled Device Software Functions: Lifecycle Management and Marketing Submission Recommendations
Draft • Jan 2025
Broader AI-device lifecycle draft. Not for implementation. Use it as emerging FDA thinking, not as final requirements.

#### FDA — Q-Submission Program
Final • May 2025
FDA encourages early engagement on proposed PCCPs through Pre-Submission/Q-Submission mechanisms, especially for complex or high-risk proposals. A Pre-Submission does not itself authorize the PCCP.

#### FDA — Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR)
Regulation • Effective Feb 2026
Current device quality-system baseline under revised 21 CFR Part 820, incorporating ISO 13485:2016 by reference.

#### FDA / Health Canada / MHRA — PCCP Guiding Principles
Guiding principles • 2023
Five principles: focused and bounded, risk-based, evidence-based, transparent, and total-product-lifecycle oriented.

#### IMDRF — Good Machine Learning Practice for Medical Device Development
Final technical document • 2025
IMDRF/AIML WG/N88 FINAL:2025. Current international GMLP principles for AI/ML-enabled medical devices.

#### FDA — Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and Application
Final guidance
Use to determine when electronic records/signatures are within Part 11 scope; avoid treating every AI/MLOps log as automatically a Part 11 record.

## Preparing for Your Exam

What do I need to have for my exam?
To ensure a smooth exam launch, please have the following items ready:

Valid Photo ID: One form of non-expired, government-issued photo identification (e.g., driver’s license, passport).

Technical Requirements: A working webcam and microphone, a strong and stable internet connection, and a compatible computer.

Environment: A quiet, private room with a hard surface to work on. Your desk or table must be clear of all other items.

Reflective Surfaces: Avoid rooms with cell phones or significant reflective surfaces.

What time should I arrive for my exam?
We recommend connecting to the exam platform 10-15 minutes before your scheduled start time. This will give you ample time to complete the identity verification and system check process without feeling rushed. The exam timer will not begin until after the exam has been officially launched.

Where am I NOT allowed to take my exam?
To ensure a secure exam environment, you may not test in certain locations. This is especially important for a high-stakes certification exam. You will NOT be permitted to test from:

A bathroom/restroom

A public space with other individuals like a coffee shop

The outdoors

A vehicle such as a car or boat

An office with glass walls

You should be seated at a hard surface like a desk or table, with your back to the main room entrance. Standing or lying down is not permitted.

Can I have children or pets with me?
No, you must be in a private, secure room alone. The presence of other people, including children, is not permitted and may result in the termination of your exam. While we love pets, they can be a distraction, so please ensure they are in a separate room during your exam session.

## During The Exam

What am I allowed and not allowed to do during my exam?
To maintain the integrity of the certification process, the following actions and behaviors will be flagged and are not permitted:

Talking aloud: Unless you have an approved accommodation, talking or whispering is not permitted.

Being out of camera view: Your face must be clearly visible in the camera frame at all times.

Anyone else in your testing area: You must be alone in your room. No one else may enter or speak to you.

Looking off-screen: Your gaze should remain on the screen.

Using unauthorized materials: You may only use materials explicitly allowed.

Taking pictures or screenshots: This is strictly forbidden. Our system will detect and block such actions.

Copying and pasting: The system will not allow you to copy or paste any exam content.

Using a secondary monitor: Only one monitor is permitted.

Can I have food or a drink during my exam?
Only clear liquids in a clear container (e.g., a glass of water, a clear water bottle with no label) are permitted. Food, smoking, and vaping are not allowed as they can cause unnecessary distractions and noise.

Are breaks allowed during my exam?
No, breaks are not permitted during the exam for any reason.

## Accommodations & Technical Support

How do I receive accommodations for my exam?
To receive accommodations, you must register your needs with the exam administrators. The Council will then submit an accommodation request on your behalf. We do not need any information about your disability. The Council needs to send us the following information about you:

First and last name

The email address you used to register for the exam

Accommodation information (e.g., extra time, bathroom breaks, permitted use of a screen reader, etc.)

This must be done prior to your exam day. If we do not have a record of your accommodations, it will delay your start process and may require rescheduling. If you have questions, please use our Contact Support form.

What should I do if I’m disconnected from my exam?
If you are disconnected, remain calm. Try to reconnect to the exam immediately. Follow the on-screen instructions to rejoin the session. If you are unable to reconnect within 5 minutes, please contact our technical support immediately for assistance. The support contact information is provided in your exam confirmation email.

## After The Exam

How do I access my exam results?
Exam results are provided directly by the Council on Pharmacy Standards, not by the proctoring service.
