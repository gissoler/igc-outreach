## Frequently asked questions

### Who funds AI research grants?
Federal agencies fund the largest share: NSF through the AI Institutes and programs like Mathematical Foundations of AI, NIH through AI-focused initiatives and the National Library of Medicine, and the Department of Energy. Private funders include Schmidt Sciences, the Sloan Foundation, the Simons Foundation, and the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, and AI companies such as Anthropic and Google run their own academic research programs.

### Can researchers outside computer science get AI grants?
Yes. Much of today&#x27;s AI funding supports applying AI to other fields, such as biology, chemistry, materials science, medicine, agriculture, and the social sciences. Several programs explicitly prefer interdisciplinary teams that pair domain scientists with machine learning researchers, so a strong scientific question can matter as much as AI expertise.

### Are there AI research grants for students and postdocs?
Yes. Beyond standard faculty grants, AI funding includes PhD and postdoctoral fellowships, industry-sponsored fellowship programs, and early career awards. Eligibility varies by program, so check each listing for career stage, citizenship, and institutional requirements.

### What is the difference between AI research grants and AI safety grants?
AI research grants cover the full field, from new methods to AI for science. AI safety grants focus on making advanced AI systems reliable and beneficial: alignment, interpretability, evaluations, security, and governance. Atom keeps a separate AI Safety Research Grants collection for those programs.
